EDITING SERVICES

The Four Stages of Professional Book Editing

Different editing stages catch different problems. Skipping one usually means the issue it would have caught makes it into your published book. Here’s what each stage covers.

Developmental Editing

A big-picture review of plot, structure, pacing, and character development (or argument and organization for nonfiction), typically done first since it can involve significant rewrites.

Line Editing

A sentence-level pass focused on voice, tone, and flow; tightening language and improving how your writing actually reads, without changing your story’s substance.

Copyediting

A technical pass for grammar, punctuation, spelling, and consistency, making sure names, timelines, and formatting stay accurate throughout the manuscript.

Proofreading

The final check before publication, catching any remaining typos or formatting slips after layout, the last set of eyes before your book goes live.

HOW WE WORK

How Your Manuscript Moves Through Editing

At BES Publisher, your manuscript progresses through structured editing stages, from detailed evaluation and revisions to proofreading and final quality checks, ensuring every page is polished, professional, and ready for publication.

STEP 01

MANUSCRIPT ASSESSMENT

We review a sample of your manuscript and discuss your goals to recommend which editing stages it actually needs, rather than defaulting everyone to the full package.

STEP 02

EDITOR MATCH & AGREEMENT

You’re matched with an editor experienced in your genre, and receive a written agreement covering scope, pricing, and timeline before work begins.

STEP 03

EDITING & REVISION ROUNDS

Your editor works through the manuscript in tracked changes, with an editorial letter for developmental edits, and a revision window for you to ask questions or request adjustments.

STEP 04

FINAL DELIVERY

You receive the fully edited manuscript, along with a clean copy ready for the next stage; whether that’s another editing pass, formatting, or publication.

FAQ’S

Questions Authors Ask About Book Editing

Clear answers to the questions authors ask most before starting book editing services with BES Publisher.

Developmental editing addresses structure, plot, and pacing. Line editing improves sentence-level flow, tone, and voice. Copyediting fixes grammar, punctuation, and consistency. Proofreading is the final pass, catching any remaining typos after formatting. Each stage catches different issues, which is why they’re typically done in that order.

Cost depends on manuscript length and which editing stages you need. Copyediting and proofreading are generally the most affordable per word, while developmental editing costs more due to the depth of analysis involved. A full four-stage edit on a novel-length manuscript typically represents the largest investment, while a single proofreading pass costs considerably less.

Not necessarily. If your manuscript has already had a solid developmental edit or you’re confident in its structure, copyediting and proofreading alone may be enough. First-time authors, or those who’ve received feedback that the story’s structure needs work, generally benefit most from starting with developmental editing.

Timelines vary by stage and manuscript length. Developmental editing typically takes the longest, given the depth of analysis involved, while proofreading is usually the fastest since it’s the lightest-touch pass. A full four-stage edit on a novel-length manuscript can take a few months from start to finish.
